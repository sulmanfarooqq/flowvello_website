# Homepage Section Gap Analysis — Flow Vello vs. Protogroup.co

Your current homepage has: Hero → Partner With Us → Core Services (4 cards) → Why Choose (qualitative) → How We Work → Closing CTA, plus a portfolio/case-study placeholder and a values section (repurposed from testimonials).

Proto's actual homepage runs more sections than that, and it reads as "complete agency" partly *because* of the extra sections, not just because of visual polish. Here's what's missing, in the order they'd appear on the page — and for each one, whether you can build it honestly right now or need to wait.

---

## 1. Industries / Who We Serve strip
**What Proto has:** a horizontal strip naming specific industry verticals (e.g. real estate, e-commerce, professional services, hospitality) with a one-line "how we help them" for each, usually right after the hero or after core services.
**Can you build it now?** Yes — this is a positioning choice, not a credibility claim. Pick 3-4 industries you can genuinely speak to (not fabricate results for, just discuss intelligently), and write one line each on the problem you'd solve for that vertical.
**Why it matters:** it's the single fastest way to make a visitor think "this is for me" instead of "this is a generic tech agency." Right now your homepage reads industry-agnostic, which reads as less experienced, even if the actual work is identical.

## 2. Expanded Core Services Grid (6-7 cards, not 4)
**What Proto has:** granular service cards — Workflow Automation, WhatsApp/Messaging Automation, AI Customer Support, Custom AI Agents, Sales Automation, Executive Dashboards, sometimes Software Development as a 7th.
**Can you build it now?** Yes, mostly a re-slicing of your existing 4 pillars into more specific, more searchable service names. "AI Agents & Automation" as one card hides two very different buyer intents (someone who wants a chatbot vs. someone who wants backend task automation) under one label. Splitting it costs you nothing and helps both SEO and clarity.
**Caution:** only add "WhatsApp & Messaging Automation" or "Sales Automation" as distinct cards if you can actually deliver them — don't copy Proto's exact 7 just to match card count.

## 3. Stat bar with real numbers (not qualitative swaps)
**What you have now:** I replaced Proto's fake "9875 clients" with qualitative labels ("Smooth," "Custom," "Ongoing"). That was the honest move for a pre-launch site — but it's also weaker than a real stat bar.
**Can you build it now?** No — not with real numbers, because you don't have clients yet. This is not a "missing section," it's a section you'll upgrade later. Don't force numbers in now. Revisit this the day you have even 2-3 completed projects with a measurable before/after.

## 4. Case Studies Preview (3 cards on the homepage, not just a hub page link)
**What Proto has:** 3 real client cards directly embedded on the homepage — name, one-line result with a number, tool used, link to full case study — not just a nav link to a case studies page.
**Can you build it now?** No. Same rule as before: an embedded fake case study on your actual homepage is a worse trust violation than a missing hub page, because it's the first thing a visitor sees, not something they have to click to find. Leave this section out of the homepage entirely until real, until you have at least one.

## 5. Testimonials (separate from your Core Values section)
**What Proto has:** real client quotes with real name/title/company, distinct from a values/mission section.
**What you have instead:** I converted the fake testimonial slot into a Core Values section as an honest stand-in. That's a permanent design decision until you have real quotes, not a temporary placeholder — don't think of it as "missing," think of it as "correctly substituted."
**When to revisit:** the moment you have one real client willing to give a quote, even a short one. One real testimonial outperforms three fabricated ones.

## 6. Process teaser with a link to a full /process page
**What Proto has:** the same 6-step "How We Work" you already have, but it links out to a dedicated process page with more depth per step (what happens in Discovery, what a Proposal includes, etc.)
**Can you build it now?** Yes, but it's a new page (`process.html`), not a homepage section — see it as a linked deep-dive from the homepage teaser you already built, not additional homepage content.

## 7. FAQ section or FAQ page link
**What Proto has:** an FAQ page, sometimes with 3-4 top questions previewed on the homepage before a "see all FAQs" link.
**Can you build it now?** Yes — this is one of the easiest wins available to you. Write 5-6 real questions a prospective client would actually ask (pricing model, timeline, do you sign NDAs, what happens after launch, do you work with our existing tools) and answer them honestly. No credibility risk here — it's your own answers to your own anticipated questions.

## 8. Trust bar / tool logos
**What Proto has:** a row of the platforms/tools they integrate with (monday.com, HubSpot, etc.) shown as logos, signaling technical range without claiming client relationships.
**Can you build it now?** Yes, carefully — showing "we work with these tools" (monday.com, Pipedrive, HubSpot, Odoo, WhatsApp Business API, whatever you actually use) is not the same claim as "these are our clients," and it's true today if you're competent in those platforms. Just don't format it in a way that implies partnership/certification you don't have (e.g. don't call it "Our Partners" unless you have an actual partner agreement with any of them).

---

## What NOT to add just because Proto has it

- **A fake "As Seen In" press logo bar.** If you haven't been covered by any publication, don't imply you have.
- **Team headshots for people who don't exist yet in your public-facing brand.** Covered already in the previous guide — still applies here.
- **A live chat widget promising instant response** if you can't actually staff one. An unanswered chat bubble is worse than no chat bubble.
- **Awards/certifications badges** unless you hold them.

---

## Priority order if you're doing this incrementally

1. Industries strip (fast, zero credibility risk, high conversion impact)
2. Expanded 6-7 card services grid (re-slicing what you already have)
3. FAQ section (fast, zero risk)
4. Tool/platform trust bar (fast, zero risk, formatted honestly)
5. Process page (`process.html`) linked from your existing teaser
6. Stat bar upgrade, testimonials, case studies preview — **only after real client work exists**, not before

The gap between your site and Proto's isn't really a design gap at this point — it's a content-maturity gap. Items 1-4 above close most of the "does this look like a complete, high-end agency" perception gap without you needing a single real client yet. Items 5-6 require the business to actually have delivered work first, and no amount of homepage polish substitutes for that.
